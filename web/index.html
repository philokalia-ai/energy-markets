    Case studies — counterfactuals on the counterfactual

    Beyond scoring, the same engine answers what-if questions. Each case study defines a scenario through the library's scenario hooks (the scenario API), re-clears a full year of Greek market days offline against the published data extract, and compares against the identically built baseline. The objective is always the same question: if X connects to the grid, how much more does everyone pay? The two runs differ only in the scenario, so the price delta is causal within the model — and because the model is a competitive counterfactual, these are competitive-market estimates. Full records, scripts and provenance: docs/experiments/scenario-exercises.

    1 · A 574 MW data center connects in Greece

    Objective. A hyperscale data center draws a constant 574 MW — about 9% of average Greek load — in every hour of the year. What does that do to the day-ahead price that every other consumer pays? Firm always-on demand belongs in the load series itself, so the scenario uses the load_modifier hook: the extra megawatts propagate into net demand, scarcity margins and hydro water values, exactly as real load would.

    GR · 2025-07-01 → 2026-06-30 hook: load_modifier runs: gr_scn_base vs gr_scn_dc574 364 days · 0 solver failures
    datacenter_574mw.jl — run it yourself against the public extract 
    using Euphemia, Dates

# scenario: +574 MW of firm, always-on demand in every timeslot
dc_load = (ts, load_mw) -> load_mw + 574.0

for day in Date(2025, 7, 1):Day(1):Date(2026, 6, 30)
    generate_energy_prices("GR", day;
        order_method  = :merit_order,
        save_to_db    = true,
        clearing_mode = "gr_scn_dc574",   # label the run
        load_modifier = dc_load)          # the scenario hook
end

# analyze: load-weighted price delta vs the baseline run
# $ julia --project=. bin/scenario_delta.jl gr_scn_base gr_scn_dc574 GR 2025-07-01 2026-07-01
    Scenario consumer costs €986m /year €983m over the 12-month window — extra spend by all Greek consumers on unchanged consumption.
    Load-weighted avg price increase +19.9% +19.46 €/MWh on the load-weighted average price (weighted by baseline hourly load).
    Everyone else pays €97.77 → €117.23 per MWh: baseline × (1 + 19.9%). The higher price applies to every MWh consumed in the zone, not just the data center's.

    Cross-check. The two headline cards are the same fact in different units: +19.9% × €97.77/MWh × ≈50.5 TWh consumed in the window ≈ €983m. The delta is weighted by baseline hourly load, so the identity is exact by construction.

    Honesty notes: €4.00 of the +19.46 €/MWh comes from just 16 scarcity hours where the added load exhausts offered supply and the price hits the €3,000 cap; the remaining +15.46 is ordinary merit-order steepening across 8,720 normal hours. Single-zone runs hold net imports at historical values — no import relief responds — so these are upper-end estimates.

    2 · Cold ironing: every ferry & cruise ship plugs in at berth

    Objective. All in-scope passenger ships at the 21 monitored Greek ports connect to shore power (onshore power supply) instead of running engines at berth. The demand profile is measured — AIS port-visit data with EMSA at-berth loads: mean 126 MW, strongly seasonal (~205–250 MW in cruise season, ~30 MW in winter, peak 369 MW). Ships connect regardless of the day-ahead price, so the scenario uses the extra_orders hook: new price-inelastic demand bid at the price cap — the real-data incarnation of the documented "ships request power" example.

    GR · 2024-07-01 → 2025-06-30 hook: extra_orders runs: gr_scn_base vs gr_scn_ops 365 days · 0 solver failures
    cold_ironing_ops.jl — run it yourself against the public extract 
    using Euphemia, Dates

# OPS_MW: measured hourly shore-power demand (MW), keyed "yyyymmdd-HH"
ops = ctx -> [SimpleOrder(:demand, 3000.0, OPS_MW[ts[1:11]],
                  Symbol(ctx.zone), DateTime(ts, dateformat"yyyymmdd-HHMM"),
                  ctx.resolution_minutes)
              for ts in ctx.timeslots if get(OPS_MW, ts[1:11], 0.0) > 0]

for day in Date(2024, 7, 1):Day(1):Date(2025, 6, 30)
    generate_energy_prices("GR", day;
        order_method  = :merit_order,
        save_to_db    = true,
        clearing_mode = "gr_scn_ops",   # label the run
        extra_orders  = ops)            # price-inelastic bids at the cap
end

# $ julia --project=. bin/scenario_delta.jl gr_scn_base gr_scn_ops GR 2024-07-01 2025-07-01
    Scenario consumer costs €148m /year €148m over the 12-month window — extra spend by all Greek consumers on unchanged consumption.
    Load-weighted avg price increase +2.8% +2.90 €/MWh on the load-weighted average price — summer-heavy: +3.62 in Jul–Dec vs +2.11 in Jan–Jun.
    Everyone else pays €102.16 → €105.06 per MWh: baseline × (1 + 2.8%). Seasonal concentration makes an OPS megawatt more expensive than a flat one: ~€1.18m/yr per MW of average load vs the data center's ~€1.72m (which includes its scarcity tail).
    Ship owners' power bill €100m /year 1.10 TWh of shore power at an OPS-weighted €90.78/MWh (scenario prices, incl. €4.3m from the increase the ships themselves cause) — wholesale energy only, no grid fees or port infrastructure. So every €1 of electricity the ships buy raises everyone else's bill by ~€1.50.

    Cross-check. +2.8% × €102.16/MWh × ≈51.1 TWh consumed in the window ≈ €148m — again the same fact in two units, exact by construction.

    Honesty notes: the profile is the central (imputed) scenario and leans high — the confirmed-ships floor is ~3.3× smaller. Full uptake assumed (every in-scope ship connects). No scenario hour reaches the price cap. Single-zone caveat as above.

    About these numbers

    This is a no-fit competitive counterfactual — a fundamentals-based market-clearing model, not a statistical forecaster. Predictions are frozen before delivery, never revised, and scored honestly as days settle. Be precise about what today's scores are: the reference track uses ENTSO-E's published D-1 forecasts and freezes in the evening — before delivery, but after the auction. The truly ex-ante track — our own weather-based wind/solar, frozen before the auction gate — is being wired in and will be scored separately. Nothing is fitted to the outcomes it is scored against, so persistent gaps between simulated and actual prices are findings about how the real market deviates from competitive behavior — not model errors to be tuned away.
