Methodology

A competitive counterfactual of the European power market

What would electricity prices be if every participant behaved competitively? We simulate the European day-ahead market from physical fundamentals alone, publish the predictions before the market clears, and score them honestly as reality settles. The gaps that persist are not model errors — they are measurements of how the real market deviates from competition.

What this is

This site tracks a no-fit, ex-ante simulation of the European day-ahead electricity market — 39 bidding zones cleared jointly, the way the real EUPHEMIA algorithm clears them, with cross-border transmission constraints. The model ingests only what a market participant could know the evening before delivery: ENTSO-E load and wind/solar forecasts, offered transfer capacities, fuel and carbon prices, reservoir levels, and the physical generation fleet. From these it builds supply and demand curves per zone, couples them over the transmission network, and solves for the market-clearing price of every hour.

No-fit means exactly that: no parameter is trained or tuned on the price outcomes the model is scored against. There is no train/serve gap to hide, which is why the backtest and the live product show the same accuracy.

Why — the research thesis

A competitive market should price at the marginal cost of the marginal unit. Our simulation is that competitive benchmark, reconstructed bottom-up. Where the real market persistently clears above the counterfactual — in specific zones, seasons, or hours — that residual is a finding about market behavior: scarcity signals, strategic bidding, or the exercise of market power. This is why we refuse to fit the model to prices: a fitted model would absorb exactly the deviations we are trying to measure.

How the model works

  1. Fleet and costs. Every dispatchable generator in each zone, with marginal costs built from real TTF gas futures, daily EUA carbon prices, and fuel emission factors — short-run marginal cost, no bid markup.
  2. The order book. Thermal units bid their marginal cost; renewables bid the D-1 forecast as price-takers; reservoir hydro bids its opportunity cost (a water value tied to gas prices, reservoir dryness and seasonal drawdown); demand is the D-1 load forecast, nearly inelastic at the cap.
  3. The coupled clear. All 39 zones clear simultaneously under cross-border capacity limits (the ex-ante flow rule uses flow climatology — never same-day observations). Zonal prices are the duals of the zonal balance constraints, exactly as in the real market coupling.
  4. Two passes. Hydro- and nuclear-anchored zones re-bid against the first pass's coupled price, so opportunity costs are consistent across the footprint.
  5. Honest inputs only. Everything is D-1-legal. When an input is not published in time, the day is skipped — never backfilled from hindsight.

The ex-ante guarantees

Every prediction on this site satisfies, by construction:

  • Frozen before delivery. An hour-level guard makes it impossible to write a value whose delivery hour has passed. Predictions are never revised; the record is append-only.
  • Provenance per row. Every prediction records the timestamp it was made, so anyone can verify what was known when.
  • Pre-auction where possible. The morning run freezes predictions before the day-ahead auction gate closes (12:00 CET) — ex-ante with respect to the market clearing itself, not just to physical delivery.
  • Complete market days only. A delivery day is a Europe/Athens market day (the 24-hour window starting 21:00/22:00 UTC the evening before, DST-aware); a day is published only when all 24 hours can be predicted.

The daily schedule

  • 00:00 UTCData fill: actuals for settled days; realized days are scored and the scoreboard updates.
  • 08:00 UTCMorning window — fill + forecast before the auction gate (10:00/11:00 UTC). Rows written here are pre-auction. On days ENTSO-E inputs have not yet propagated, the run honestly writes nothing.
  • 17:30 UTCEvening fill — ENTSO-E's D-1 items propagate to the bulk export late for large zones (~19:00 UTC for Germany and Italy), so the evening run fills whatever the morning could not see. Writes never overwrite a morning row.

The 7-day horizon and the vintage record

The Next 7 days view publishes an hourly price forecast for every upcoming market day. Because the decisive ENTSO-E inputs exist only for tomorrow, the leads are honest about their information:

  • D−1 is the full model forecast on complete published inputs — frozen the evening before delivery.
  • D−2 … D−7 are currently weekly persistence of the model: the frozen model forecast from the same weekday one week earlier. A deliberately simple baseline — when the weather layer lands, each lead becomes a true re-forecast on fresh weather, and the per-lead scoreboard will show the improvement against exactly this baseline.

Every day the pipeline writes the newly-due vintage for each future day and never overwrites an earlier one — so for every delivery day D the record holds what we said at D−7, D−6, …, D−1, each stamped with the moment it was made. The "What we said, when" panel shows exactly that: all vintages overlaid, oldest faintest, against the settled actual once the day realizes. Forecast revisions are data, not embarrassments — they are how the model's information gain per day of lead time is measured.

Scoring, honestly

As days settle, predictions are scored per zone and per lead time against the official day-ahead auction prices: MAE (mean absolute error, €/MWh), bias (mean simulated − actual — negative means the model under-prices, which for a competitive counterfactual is a signal, not a bug), and correlation of hourly prices. Whole-sample, never cherry-picked. On the full-year EU backtest the model reaches a mean hourly correlation of 0.56 across 39 zones (Germany 0.85, Greece 0.86) with MAE €30/MWh and near-zero aggregate bias; zones with correlation above 0.7 cover roughly 60% of European traded volume. Weak zones (the Nordic hydro north, some small zones) are shown with the same prominence as strong ones.

The weather layer — becoming independent of late inputs

The model's decisive inputs — ENTSO-E's day-ahead load and renewables forecasts — are published late in the day and only for the next day, which caps the horizon at lead 1. To extend it, we predict wind and solar ourselves from raw weather forecasts: a GFS + ECMWF + ICON ensemble evaluated at wind-farm-sited grid cells (an OpenStreetMap extraction of 115,390 turbines across the footprint, validated at 97% against the Greek ELETAEN registry), and solar from irradiance plus sun geometry. Measured on strictly honest lead-1 forecast vintages, the weather layer predicts ENTSO-E's own wind forecast at correlation 0.96 and its solar forecast at 0.99 — and losing only ~0.01 at lead 2 means lead 2–7 forecasts inherit near-lead-1 quality. This is what will make the morning window productive every day and extend the site beyond tomorrow.

What we tested and rejected

Negative results are part of the record: inter-temporal commitment bidding (block orders with startup dynamics) was built, tested across 14 zones, and rejected — it helps only thermal-cycling zones in winter and hurts hydro-dominated ones; a plant-level physical simulation of renewables was evaluated and found unnecessary (one sun-geometry feature carries the physics). The full investigation record lives in the repository's docs/.

Open by default

The model, the calibration history, the experiment scripts, and a self-contained public data extract are on GitHub — philokalia-ai/energy-markets. The full 39-zone clear reproduces offline from the published artifact, no database required. Data: ENTSO-E Transparency Platform, TTF/EUA via public market feeds, OpenStreetMap (turbine locations), open-meteo (weather).
