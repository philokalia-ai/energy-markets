Model lines

Beyond the physics: the pink and yellow lines

The solid blue line on every chart is the physics counterfactual — a competitive re-clearing of the European day-ahead market from fundamentals (fleets, fuels, forecasts, ATC coupling of the 39 zones — on flow-based borders JAO's maximum bilateral exchanges stand in as capacities; the full PTDF/RAM domain is not modelled). No price is ever a regression target: the bidding form's named characteristics (about 40 — scarcity onsets, water values, the tranche ladder) were chosen with price error in view on calibration windows — most later checked on held-out days inside the same historical period, the two newest without a separate hold-out — and the code flags every one of them as price-informed. Its job is to be a defensible counterfactual, not the best point forecast. The dashed lines are its honest statistical companions. This page explains what they are, what they are good for, and what they can never do.

The three models

linemodelwhat it isfitted on prices?
Physics (solid blue) The Euphemia-style competitive clearing of all 39 zones from ex-ante fundamentals. Not regressed on prices. Its ~40 named characteristics were calibrated on price error; no price is a regression target.
Physics + ex-ante GBM (dashed pink) The physics price plus a per-zone gradient-boosted model of the residual (settled − physics), trained only on features known before the 12:00 CET gate: the order book's demand, RES/import shares and margin, D-2 fuel closes, calendar. It is trained on the cv37 physics line, so it is drawn only on days that have a cv37 physics base — not on days produced by the current model. Yes — on the residual.
Pure-stats GBM (dashed yellow) No physics at all: a per-zone gradient-boosted model of the settled price itself, from lagged settled prices (24 h / 48 h / 168 h, weekly mean), calendar, fuels, and the published D-1 load & RES forecasts. The strongest honest "just use statistics" baseline. Yes — directly.
Timestral TSFM (dashed violet, shaded q10–q90) A pretrained time-series foundation model (Mistral's Timestral-209M), used zero-shot, forecasting tomorrow's hourly price. It runs twice on D-1. The 06:30 UTC run reads the settled price history alone and is published before the 12:00 CET gate. The 10:40 UTC refresh adds our frozen lead-1 physics forecast as a known-future input; its inputs are capped at the latest forecast vintage made by 10:00 UTC (its issue time), but the run itself — and its appearance on the site at about 11:30 UTC — comes after the summer gate (12:00 CEST = 10:00 UTC), and the publication is after the winter gate (11:00 UTC) too. So the 10:40 line is a forecast on pre-gate inputs, not a pre-gate publication. Its 21 quantiles also give P(price ≤ 0) per hour, shown averaged 50/50 with an hourly logistic model of collapse (refit on settled ≤ 0 hours). The negative-price risk badge (shown at 30 % or more) is a separate day-level blend: 50/50 of the highest hourly Timestral P(≤ 0) and a day-level logistic model of collapse days, fitted separately. Not fine-tuned on our data — but it reads settled prices (and, at 10:40, the physics forecast), and both logistic models in the risk blends are trained on settled prices. A prediction, never part of the counterfactual.

Timestral backtest (2026-09-14 → 11-13, 58 days × 39 zones, lead 1; zero-shot, so each day is forecast only from the history before it — no training split; target: the settled price as the live scorer read it before 2026-11-17, which for AT and DE_LU was the EXAA auction; not yet rerun on the SDAC target now used for all hourly scores): MAE 28.0 €/MWh from prices only (06:30, 54k hours); 25.3 with the physics forecast as input on the 47,679 cells where a forecast vintage existed by 10:00 UTC — the days the 10:40 refresh can actually publish (25.1 on a broader reconstruction that also includes seven days whose only vintage was made after 10:00, which production refuses); hourly P(≤ 0) blend Brier 0.009, AUC 0.97. It extrapolates statistically, so — like the yellow line — it lags regime breaks and forecasts collapses mostly where recent history shows them. Timestral weights are used under their Modified-MIT licence.

How good is each?

The strict test: rolling origin (2026-11, cv39). Built on the cv39 books and the cv39 physics record. Each of the four quarters 2025-07-01 → 2026-06-30 is predicted only from data before it (training from 2024-07-01): chronological, 39 zones, 341,256 zone-hours, scored against the canonical SDAC settlement (for AT and DE_LU the SDAC auction, not EXAA). MAE is the plain mean over all cells, pooled across the four folds.

Who wins where

Under the strict rolling-origin test the hybrid beats physics in all four quarters only in IT-NORTH, IT-CNORTH, IT-CSOUTH, IT-SOUTH, IT-Calabria, SE1 and SE2, and loses heavily in BG, HU, RO and AT; the stats line beats physics in 105 of 156 zone-quarters. On the older non-chronological table, the hybrid (pink) wins where the physics is strongest — the continental core (DE/FR/BE/NL/AT/CH/CZ: MAE 13.2 vs stats 13.5), Poland–Slovakia–Hungary, the Baltics — and ties in the Nordics. The stats line (yellow) wins where the physics has a known structural gap — Italy (9.7 vs 12.7) and Iberia — because a lag model isn't dragged by the model's bias shape. Physics keeps the best spike precision everywhere north of the Alps (0.84–1.00): when the blue line calls an extreme hour, it is calling real, mechanical scarcity — not pattern-matching.

What the dashed lines can never do

  • Measure conduct. The stats model learns the market's actual bidding behaviour — including any exercise of market power — into its lags; it can only ever agree with the market. The physics line is the benchmark the market is compared against, with one caveat: its bidding form includes a declared conduct layer (the upper tranche ladder and the scarcity/peak markups) whose values were chosen with price error in view. Switching that layer off (EUPHEMIA_CONDUCT_LAYER=off, conduct-layers-367) is a sensitivity check under the current opportunity-cost layer, not an identified competitive benchmark. So the persistent gap between blue and settled in tight evening hours (Hungary, Poland, the Baltics) is a finding to explain, not a measured markup.
  • Handle a regime they never saw. A fuel shock, a new interconnector, a war outage move the physics line correctly on day one; a lag model needs weeks of new history.
  • Explain themselves. Every blue-line price decomposes into the order book on the book page. The dashed lines are predictions, not explanations.

Honesty notes

  • The older table's 14.9 for the pink line (and 14.5 / 0.52 / 0.49 beside it) comes from non-chronological GroupKFold cross-validation that trains on later days — held-out, but flattering. The rolling-origin numbers (cv39: 23.30 / 24.70 / 21.05) are the headline. The physics line is never regressed on prices and uses only ex-ante inputs, but its calibrated characteristics were chosen on windows inside this same period, so its number is not a strict hold-out either.
  • Because calibration overlaps every historical evaluation window, the only clean out-of-sample record of the physics line is the live one since its calibrated characteristics were frozen with cv44 on 2026-11-15; any later change to one of them gets a new code version and restarts that clock. The scoreboard shows it as the window “since freeze (cv44, 2026-11-15)”. It is still very short, so read it as a running tally, not yet a verdict.
  • The pink-line features are chosen to be gate-legal (nothing after D-1 12:00 CET), and the yellow line's lagged prices are auction results published up to D-2 12:45 CET. From 2026-11-17, GBM rows are issued before the gate and are not rewritten afterwards; older rows were rewritten daily. Physics and settled-price vintages are limited to issue time.
  • Where a day has no dashed lines, the models had no validated series for it — gaps are shown, never interpolated.

Scenario counterfactuals and the model lines

The scenario case studies (the data-center, cold-ironing and bring-your-own-power exercises) take their delta from physics alone: a scenario compares two competitive clearings by the same engine, and only the physics engine can clear a world that never happened. Where a scenario is shown as a forecast (tomorrow's price with the data center connected, say), it is the physics-delta composite: the Timestral base forecast plus Euphemia's Δ (scenario − base) for each zone-hour. It is never Timestral's own response to the change — a statistical model reacts to a structural shift unreliably (0.1× to 1.9× the physics Δ in our tests). The case-study figures are labelled with the model version that produced them; the cv43 data-center, bring-your-own-power and cold-ironing figures were re-checked on the current model (cv44, stride-8 sample) and hold (e.g. s* ≈ 192 % on cv44 vs 193 % on cv43).

Full methodology and per-zone tables: docs/experiments/forecast-eval-2026-08. The evaluation window is 2024-07-01 → 2026-06-30; the dashed series on recent days are true out-of-period predictions from models frozen on that window.
