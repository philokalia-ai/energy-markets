Models

The forecast: Timestral + physics

Every chart shows two models. The forecast is Timestral + physics: a time-series foundation model that reads the settled price history and our physics clear of the delivery day. The physics clear (cv45) is the competitive counterfactual itself: a re-clearing of the 39-zone European day-ahead market from fundamentals. The forecast is the best point prediction we have; the physics is the benchmark the market is measured against. Headline: rMAE 0.578 vs the D-1 naive over two years of the live configuration (details below).

The models on the charts

Timestral + physics: the forecast

Mistral's pretrained Timestral-209M, used zero-shot. Its inputs are the settled day-ahead prices up to the start of the delivery day, plus our frozen lead-1 physics clear of that day as a known-future input (the latest vintage made by 10:00 UTC on D−1). It gives a median and a q10–q90 band per hour. It is hourly: on a day the physics clears in 15-minute periods it reads the clear's hourly means, and the charts draw it as an hourly step under the 15-minute physics. It is not trained on our data, but it reads settled prices: a prediction, never part of the counterfactual. If the physics-conditioned issue is missing for a day, the chart shows that day's 06:30 UTC prices-only issue.

Physics (cv45): the counterfactual

The competitive clearing of all 39 zones from ex-ante fundamentals: the per-unit fleet at short-run marginal cost (gas on the TTF gross-calorific basis, daily EUA carbon), reservoir hydro at its water value, the D-1 load and RES forecasts, and the coupled network (offered ATC; JAO's maximum bilateral exchanges on flow-based borders). No price is a regression target; about 40 named bidding characteristics were chosen with price error in view and are flagged as price-informed.

≤ 0 risk

The probability that a day has at least one hour at or below 0 €/MWh, shown as a badge at 30 % or more: a 50/50 blend of Timestral's quantile P(≤ 0) and a per-zone D-1 logistic model of collapse, refit on settled ≤ 0 hours.

How good is it

rMAE is the forecast's mean absolute error divided by that of the D-1 naive (the settled price of the same hour one day earlier), on the same hours; below 1 beats it. Scored against the SDAC day-ahead price, lead 1.

  • The live configuration: rMAE 0.578. Timestral conditioned on the weather-track lead-1 physics (our own 06:30 weather inputs, as the site's forecast uses), Athens market days 2024-11-16 → 2026-11-15: 718 days × 39 zones, 671,328 hours on which every compared model has a value (12 days have no physics run). MAE 14.5 €/MWh against a naive MAE of 25.0, bias −0.8; by region from 0.530 (Nordic) to 0.662 (Italy). The physics in this backtest is cv44.
  • With physics on the TSOs' D-1 inputs: rMAE 0.555. Timestral conditioned on the cv45 record physics (ENTSO-E D-1 load and RES forecasts at their latest revision, a gate-time information set), same window: 729 days × 39 zones, 679,848 common hours.

Zero-shot means no training split: each day is forecast only from the history before it. Live scores per zone and window are on the scoreboard.

Why the physics stays on the chart

  • It measures conduct. A statistical forecast learns the market's actual bidding, including any exercise of market power, from the prices it reads; it can only agree with the market. The physics line is the benchmark the market is compared against. One qualification: its bidding form includes a declared conduct layer (the upper tranche ladder and the scarcity/peak markups) chosen with price error in view. Switching it off (EUPHEMIA_CONDUCT_LAYER=off) is a sensitivity check, not an identified competitive price. So a persistent gap between physics and settled prices is a finding to explain, not a measured markup.
  • It handles a regime it never saw. A fuel shock, a new interconnector or a large outage moves the physics on day one. A model that reads price history lags regime breaks and forecasts collapses mostly where recent history shows them.
  • It explains itself. Every physics price decomposes into the order book on the book page.

Timing and inputs

  • The forecast uses settled prices strictly before the delivery day and a physics vintage made by 10:00 UTC on D−1. It reaches the site after the auction gate, so it is a forecast on pre-gate inputs, not a pre-gate publication.
  • The physics forecasts are frozen when made and never revised. Their calibrated characteristics are frozen too: any change gets a new code version and restarts the scoreboard's “since freeze” window, the only clean out-of-sample physics record.
  • Where a day has no forecast line, none was issued for it: gaps are shown, never interpolated.
  • Timestral weights are used under their Modified-MIT licence.
