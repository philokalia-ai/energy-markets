Beyond the physics: the pink and yellow lines

The blue line on every day chart is the physics counterfactual — a competitive re-clearing of the European day-ahead market from fundamentals (fleets, fuels, forecasts, JAO flow-based network), with zero fitted parameters against prices. Its job is to be a defensible counterfactual, not the best point forecast. The two dashed lines are honest statistical companions, and this page explains what they are, what they are good for, and what they can never do.

The three models

linemodelwhat it isfitted on prices?
Physics (solid blue) The Euphemia-style competitive clearing of all 39 zones from ex-ante fundamentals. No — zero parameters fitted to prices.
Physics + ex-ante GBM (dashed pink) The physics price plus a per-zone gradient-boosted model of the residual (settled − physics), trained only on features known before the 12:00 CET gate: the order book's demand, RES/import shares and margin, D-2 fuel closes, calendar. Yes — on the residual.
Pure-stats GBM (dashed yellow) No physics at all: a per-zone gradient-boosted model of the settled price itself, from lagged settled prices (24 h / 48 h / 168 h, weekly mean), calendar, fuels, and the published D-1 load & RES forecasts. The strongest honest "just use statistics" baseline. Yes — directly.

Who wins where

The hybrid (pink) wins where the physics is strongest — the continental core (DE/FR/BE/NL/AT/CH/CZ, MAE 13.2 vs stats 13.5), Poland–Slovakia–Hungary, the Baltics, and it ties in the Nordics. The stats line (yellow) wins where the physics has a known structural gap — Italy (9.7 vs 12.7) and Iberia — because a lag model isn't dragged by the model's bias shape. Physics keeps the best spike precision everywhere north of the Alps (0.84–1.00): when the blue line calls an extreme hour, it is calling real, mechanical scarcity — not pattern-matching.

What the dashed lines can never do

  • Measure conduct. The stats model learns the market's actual bidding behaviour — including any exercise of market power — into its lags. It can only ever agree with the market. The physics line is the competitive benchmark the market can be compared against; the persistent gap between blue and settled in tight evening hours (Hungary, Poland, the Baltics) is a finding, not an error.
  • Handle a regime it never saw. A fuel shock, a new interconnector, a war outage move the physics line correctly on day one. A lag model needs weeks of new history.
  • Explain itself. Every blue-line price decomposes into the order book you can open on the book page. The dashed lines are predictions, not explanations.

Honesty notes

  • Both dashed lines are evaluated out-of-sample (5-fold cross-validation grouped by day), but the folds shuffle days rather than respecting time order — a strict walk-forward evaluation would score both ML lines somewhat worse. The physics line has no fitting and no leakage of any kind.
  • The pink line's features are all gate-legal (nothing after D-1 12:00 CET enters); the yellow line's lagged prices are the auction results published up to D-2 12:45 CET — also gate-legal.
  • Where a day has no dashed lines, the models had no validated series for it — gaps are shown, never interpolated.
